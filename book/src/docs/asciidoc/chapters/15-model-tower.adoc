[#model-tower]
== The Model Tower

[horizontal]
Capability:: A set of small models of an organization and its solutions that build on each other, so that an element at the top is documented, owned, access controlled, staged, decided, governed, worked on and threat-modeled without any one model defining more than its own concern.
Demonstration:: The Nasdanika International Banking System, as it grows. Today the tower is published as the pre-SDK models, each with its own site.
Status:: [.planned]#PLANNED#, floor by floor. The 22 models are in `models/tower` as Xcore files to read, as they are in the pre-SDK tower.
Depends on:: <<core-nano-models>>, and Meta for the upper floors.
Value and effort:: The largest item in this book, and the slowest. Its value is in composition, so it arrives a floor at a time.
How to help:: Take the floor nearest to your work and model something real with it, on the pre-SDK version if need be. What a model cannot say is the most useful thing to learn before it is rebuilt.

=== What the tower is

The tower is not a product suite.
It is a set of small models, each published on its own and each depending only on those below it.
What makes it more than a pile is that the floors compose.
A concern defined once near the bottom, such as identity, documentation, provenance or time, is inherited by everything above, and a model that takes its place in the tower stops defining what a lower floor already provides.
The tower gets taller and the models get smaller.

Taken together they describe an organization from a button on a screen down to a field in a record of a mainframe, with ownership, access, stage, decisions, risks, controls, evidence, cost and work along the way.
One graph then answers questions that today take four tools and an export: which teams depend on this system of record, who approved this alternative and on what analysis, which controls cover this requirement, what breaks if this library has a vulnerability.

It is for anyone who has to understand something complicated quickly and pass it on accurately.
A model is a deliverable that keeps working after the engagement that produced it: it can be handed over, extended, queried and regenerated.
A reference architecture, a set of controls and a catalog of personas are models too, and new work starts from one of them and not from an empty page.

=== What changes with the SDK

The tower was built on the older Nasdanika, floor by floor, in Xcore.
Three things change as it moves.

*The lowest floors become the core.*
What the tower called NxCore is the runtime's core model.
Time leaves it for the occupancy nano-model, where it is one dimension among others.
Role, IAM, Seal and Lifecycle become nano-models.
So the tower proper starts higher than it did, on a base that more models share.

*Concepts move down, and models shrink.*
With relationships, kinds, occupancy and the enterprise vocabulary in core, several floors lose most of what they define.
An architecture element with relationships is nearly `Related`.
A persona with concerns is a stakeholder with concerns.
A model is expected to be smaller after the move than before it, and where it is not, something below it is wrong.

*The upper floors are defined in Meta.*
Up to lifecycle the models stay hand-written Xcore: a small, slow base that everything cites.
From accounting upward a floor is defined in Meta, the runtime's language-neutral type system, and its Xcore, Ecore and TypeScript are generated.
The floors above lifecycle are structure with almost no behavior, which is what Meta describes.

*A product is built on a projection, not on the tower.*
Someone building a product for resumes needs a person, positions over time, organizations and skills, and not eight levels of supertypes.
They select the elements they need, flatten the hierarchies they do not care about, and get a small model generated for them, in which every class and feature records the tower element it came from.
When the tower changes, the projection is generated again and compared, and an empty difference says the product is not affected.
Large reference vocabularies have been cut down to subsets this way for twenty years.
The tool that does it, an assembler, is [.planned]#PLANNED# after the browser form of the polymorphic user interface.

=== One user interface or many

The tower's models will have user interfaces in the terminal first, and accounting is the one to start with, because its author is its first user.

The form is not one application for everything.
It is a navigator with contributions: the tree of accounts on one side, and on the other a view that depends on what is selected, an account's entries, a transaction, an assertion that a balance is what the bank says it is.
Each view comes from the module that knows the class, as views do everywhere in the runtime.
The same form serves the other floors: a tree of capabilities, of controls, of work.
It is the navigator with details, which is now <<explorer>>.

=== Statements arrive

[horizontal]
Capability:: A ledger that takes in what a bank publishes, wherever it shows up: a file that a person downloads, a message that says a statement is ready, and, if ever, a feed. Every balance that arrives becomes an assertion with its source, every transaction an entry in the state `imported`, and what does not reconcile becomes something to do.
Demonstration:: A synthetic household ledger: statements downloaded as OFX, notification messages from the same institutions, and a slipped balance that verification finds by interval and account. My own ledger is the first real use, and stays private.
Status:: [.planned]#PLANNED#. Nothing is built. The pre-SDK `accounting.xcore` already has `Source`, `EntryState` and `Assertion`, which is most of what this needs.
Depends on:: The accounting floor, a volume that reports changes, and, for the agent, <<agentic-components>>.
Value and effort:: A reader of OFX and a command that imports a file are small, need no inference, and give most of the value. Watching a folder is small once a volume reports changes. Messages are moderate. A direct connection to banks is large, fragile and a liability, and is not planned. None of it moves the accounting floor up the order: it is the floor's first demonstration, and waits for it.
How to help:: Tell me which of your institutions put a balance and a date in their notification messages, and which offer OFX or QFX, CSV, or only PDF. No account numbers.

The idea came from a message from a bank saying that a statement was ready.
Three things can follow such a message, and they are worth very different amounts.

==== A file a person downloads

The person signs in, with whatever second factor the bank asks for, and downloads a statement or a range of transactions.
The ledger sees the file appear and takes it in.
Nothing holds a password, and nothing breaks when the bank redesigns its site.

OFX carries more than transactions, and so does QFX, which is OFX with an identifier for Intuit's products.
A statement has a ledger balance with the date it holds as of, so every download asserts a balance with no extra work, and verification gets a fixed point at the end of every period downloaded.
Each transaction has an identifier from the institution, unique within the account, so periods that overlap are taken in once.
CSV has neither, and needs a mapping per institution and a balance entered by hand.
A PDF is the last resort, and the first place where a language model earns its keep.

This is the route Quicken has called Web Connect for decades, and the one that plain text accounting automates.
Beancount's importers identify the files in a folder of downloads and extract transactions and balance assertions from them, and hledger's `import` remembers what it has already seen.
What is new here is not the import.
It is that what is imported stays in the state `imported`, with its source, until a person reviews it, and that a balance that does not hold is reported with the interval and the account where it slipped.

==== A message that says a statement is ready

A notification is a claim, not a statement.
For a deposit account it usually says only that a statement is ready.
For a card it often carries the statement balance, the minimum payment and the due date.

So a message does one of two things.
With a balance and a date, it is an assertion whose source is the message.
Without them, it is an expectation: a source with no entries yet, which the downloaded file fills later, so that a statement announced and never downloaded is something the ledger can list.
When a message and a file assert a balance for the same account and date, they corroborate each other.
When they disagree, that is a finding, which is what an assertion with a source is for.

Notification messages are what phishing imitates, and a message is text that an agent would read.
So the rules are narrow.
Only messages from senders that the ledger names are read, filtered by the mail server, and only with a valid DKIM signature, which the source records.
Nothing else in the mailbox is visible: the bound is the view, not the agent's good behavior, as in <<target-ai>>.
Nothing follows a link or signs in anywhere.
Each institution's format is a mapping written once, and a language model is asked only about a message that no mapping recognizes.
Either way the result is the same typed entry in the state `imported`, checked against the model, and a person confirms it.

A mail folder fits the runtime as a read-only volume, in which a message is a file.
A new message and a new download then arrive through the same mechanism, and whatever watches does not care which it was.

==== A connection to the bank

Reading the bank directly is what the large personal finance applications do, through aggregators such as Plaid, MX, Finicity and Yodlee.
Self-hosted tools such as Actual Budget and Firefly III do it through cheaper bridges such as SimpleFIN, and in Europe GoCardless under PSD2.
OFX Direct Connect survives at some banks, for a fee at some of them.
Signing in with a browser driven by Playwright, which the runtime has, would work for one bank for a while.

It is not planned.
An aggregator is an account with an outside party that sees every transaction.
Scraping means storing credentials, getting past a second factor that exists for a reason, and breaking terms of service.
Either is a product of its own.
If a connection ever comes, it comes as an adapter that writes the same files into the same folder, so that nothing after the folder changes.
In the United States, access to one's own data through standard interfaces is the subject of a rule under section 1033 of the Dodd-Frank Act, which has been in flux since 2025.
If it settles, such an adapter gets cheaper.

==== Where an agent fits

Most of this needs no inference: reading a file, matching a sender, folding entries between assertions.
That is the rule of <<direction>>, to show first what needs no inference.
An agent earns a place at three points.
It proposes the other leg of a transaction, the expense account for a payment to a merchant, from the history of the ledger, as Beancount's smart_importer does with a classifier.
It reads a PDF or a message that no mapping knows.
And when a balance does not hold, it looks at the entries in the failing interval for a duplicate, a missing entry or a leg posted to the wrong account, and says which it suspects.
Each answer is a proposal, an entry in a state, that a person accepts or rejects.
In the terms of <<agentic-components>>, the agent requests a language model and can be given none, in which case the ledger works as before, with more for the person to do.

==== More than a convenience

It is the North Star in miniature.
An event arrives, an agent turns it into typed claims with their sources, verification checks them against what is known, and a person confirms, with the ledger in Git so that any of it can be rolled back.
That is agentic work on a bounded, typed model, with real data every month and a first user who is also the author.

It is also the other side of the North Star's slice.
The review of imported entries is where a customer finds a transaction they do not recognize, which is where the dispute of a card transaction begins.
The customer's ledger and the International Banking System are the two parties to one transaction, and the demonstration of a dispute could start from an entry marked as not recognized, a state the sketch does not have yet.
That is a candidate, not a decision.

==== Steps

. A reader of OFX and QFX, and a command that imports a file: transactions in the state `imported`, the ledger balance as an assertion, duplicates skipped by the institution's identifier.
. Watching a folder of downloads, once a volume reports changes.
. Messages: a mail folder as a volume, a mapping per sender, assertions and expectations.
. Proposals from an agent: the other leg, formats no mapping knows, the search for a slip.

A connection to a bank is not among them.

=== Order

The tower moves in the order of dependency, and a floor moves when something needs it.

. The nano-models of <<core-nano-models>>, since everything stands on them.
. Role, IAM, Seal and Lifecycle as nano-models.
. Accounting, as the first floor in Meta and the first with a terminal interface, with the intake of statements as its first demonstration: <<accounting-intake>>.
. Then by need. The International Banking System decides: what a bank's example requires next is what moves next.

A floor reaches Maven Central when its namespace is settled, it has a book, and something other than itself uses it.
Until then its sketch is here.
